Admissions: the high school's five questions go to the student

Version 14 took the "Student Section" out of the inquiry form, because a parent
typing answers on behalf of a teenager who is not in the room is worth less than
asking nobody. This is the other half: the student gets the questions in an
email of their own, answers them in their own words, and the answers land on the
admissions case where staff read everything else.

The form already promises exactly this — "Your student will be sent a few
questions for him/her to complete once you submit this form." Until now that was
a promise nothing kept.

No account. A fourteen-year-old will not create one, and requiring it would cost
more answers than it protects. The link follows the token design in migration
230: 256 bits minted in the application, only the SHA-256 digest stored, both
the resolve and the submit behind SECURITY DEFINER functions that fail closed
and hand back the student's first name and the school's name and nothing else.

The database keeps its own copy of the five accepted keys and caps each answer,
because this runs for anyone holding a link. A test reads the migration and
checks the two lists still agree.

Also in this ship: v14 itself, which removes that section, relabels the
student's email address to say what it is for, and asks the high school the
scholarship question Virtual already asked.
